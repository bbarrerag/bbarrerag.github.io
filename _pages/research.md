## Non-adiabatic Dynamics: Moving Beyond the Born-Oppenheimer Approximation

Many systems in nature exhibit phenomena occurring on widely differing timescales. This so-called \emph{timescale separation} between a set of slow vs. fast components often allows us to simplify the description of complex systems. An everyday example is a swinging bucket of water. If the bucket swings slowly, we can assume the water surface remains nearly horizontal, remaining in instantaneous equilibrium at every instant of time.  

A century ago, Born and Oppenheimer applied this simple idea to molecular systems, where a similar timescale separation arises due to the difference between the nuclear and electronic masses. Today, the [Born–Oppenheimer approximation](cite original BO paper) (BOA) remains a foundation method across both quantum chemistry and condensed matter physics. However, it often fails, and there is a large interest in developing techniques to go beyond the adiabatic limit. 

This project is my long effort throughout my PhD to develop ways to think about going beyond the Born-Oppenheimer limit. Drawing from ideas connecting quantum geometry and non-adiabatic response, we developed a systematic way to extend the BOA, which we termed the [Moving Born-Oppenheimer approximation](cite MBO paper). Like in the BOA, the fast degrees of freedom follow an equilibrium state dependent on the slow ones. Unlike the BOA, they are not in instantaneous equilibrium, but in what we call ``moving" or MBO equilibrium. 

We applied this spin entanglement, squeezing, and synchronized time-dependent states through motion.
